# What Is Retatrutide, and Why Is It Getting Attention?

Retatrutide is an experimental medicine being studied for obesity and other conditions. It has attracted attention for the amount of weight participants have lost in clinical trials and for an approach that acts on three hormone receptors. Eli Lilly, its developer, is studying it as a weekly injection. [Retatrutide overview](https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide)

**As of October 19, 2026, retatrutide is not FDA-approved.** Its research results can help us understand a possible future treatment, but there is no approved retatrutide prescription available today. [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss)

## What does “triple agonist” mean?

Hormones send signals by attaching to receptors on cells. An agonist is a substance that activates a receptor. Retatrutide is one molecule designed to activate receptors for three hormones: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. That is why researchers call it a triple agonist. You may also encounter the nickname “GLP-3,” but that is not its scientific classification. [How retatrutide is described](https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide)

The glucagon component is part of the scientific interest. In early experiments in mice, retatrutide's effects included eating less and using more energy, with glucagon-receptor activity contributing to the energy-use effect. These findings helped explain the rationale for developing the medicine. They do not establish how much extra energy a person would use or prove that three targets must work better than two. [Original development research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35985340/)

For that, researchers need human trials that measure benefits and risks together.

## The weight-loss results behind the attention

The Phase 3 TRIUMPH-1 study randomly assigned 2,339 adults without diabetes to retatrutide or placebo for 80 weeks. Participants had obesity or overweight with a weight-related condition. The trial was funded by Lilly, and its main report was published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in September 2026. [Published study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42814954/), [study population](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss-in-pivotal-phase-3-obesity-trial-302778859.html)

In the analysis that accounted for participants regardless of whether they stayed on treatment, the highest-dose group, assigned 12 mg weekly, lost an estimated average of **25.0% of starting body weight**, compared with **3.9% with placebo**. The lower-dose groups also lost weight, with smaller average reductions. These are study results, not recommended doses or predictions for an individual. [TRIUMPH-1 results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42814954/)

Losing roughly a quarter of starting weight explains why this research has drawn interest. Understanding the result also means looking at who was studied, how long treatment lasted, and which analysis produced the number.

## Why some headlines say 28.3%

Lilly also reported a 28.3% average reduction for the highest-dose group, versus 2.2% with placebo, using a different analysis. It estimates what would happen if participants stayed on the study treatment without starting prohibited weight-management treatments. The 25.0% estimate counts outcomes regardless of whether participants discontinued the study medicine or started other weight-management treatments. [Lilly's results and analysis definitions](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss-in-pivotal-phase-3-obesity-trial-302778859.html)

Both analyses can be informative. The difference is a reason to read beyond the largest number in a headline. Neither tells you what your own result would be.

## Side effects are part of the result

Lilly reported nausea, diarrhea, constipation, and vomiting among the common adverse events in TRIUMPH-1. It also reported dysesthesia—unusual or unpleasant sensations, such as altered skin sensitivity. In the highest-dose group, 11.3% stopped treatment because of adverse events, compared with 4.9% receiving placebo. [Reported safety findings](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss-in-pivotal-phase-3-obesity-trial-302778859.html)

Those experiences matter alongside the average weight loss. A treatment's usefulness depends partly on whether people can continue it, and an encouraging trial cannot rule out every uncommon or long-term risk. Retatrutide's safety is still being evaluated; there is no FDA-approved prescribing label that settles its uses, precautions, and dosing. [Current development status](https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide)

## Is it better than tirzepatide?

TRIUMPH-1 compared retatrutide with placebo. It did not test retatrutide against tirzepatide or semaglutide. Putting percentages from separate trials next to each other does not create a direct comparison: the participants, duration, doses, and analysis can differ. [TRIUMPH-1 design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42814954/)

A separate trial, TRIUMPH-5, is comparing retatrutide with tirzepatide. When checked on October 19, 2026, its registry listed it as active, with enrollment closed and no results posted. That comparison is one of the developments to watch as the evidence grows. [TRIUMPH-5 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06662383?intr=retatrutide&tab=results)

## What about retatrutide offered online?

A website offering retatrutide does not mean it has become an approved treatment. FDA states that retatrutide cannot be used in compounding under federal law and warns against products sold to consumers under labels such as “for research purposes.” Their quality is unknown. [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss)

Lilly says access to its investigational medicine is through its clinical trials. Participation depends on a study's eligibility requirements and whether it is recruiting. [Developer's access information](https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide), [example trial requirements](https://trials.lilly.com/en-US/trial/549215)

If you are considering weight treatment now, a clinician can help you discuss the options already available and what fits your health history. Retatrutide is worth following as research develops. A future treatment possibility does not have to put today's care conversation on hold.
