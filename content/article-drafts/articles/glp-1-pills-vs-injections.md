# GLP-1 Pills vs. Injections: What’s Different?

If a weight-loss medicine comes as a pill, does that make the decision simple? For someone who dislikes needles, it may seem that way.

The route matters, but so do the ingredient, approved use, daily routine, other medicines, and side effects. Two pills can have more practical differences than a pill and an injection.

The options have also changed. This overview reflects US product information checked on October 19, 2026.

## There is more than one kind of pill

Wegovy contains semaglutide and is available in tablet and injection formulations. Foundayo contains orforglipron, a different GLP-1 receptor agonist. The FDA approved Foundayo in April 2026 for long-term weight management in eligible adults, alongside diet and physical activity. [FDA Foundayo approval announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-new-molecular-entity-under-national-priority-voucher-program).

Zepbound contains tirzepatide, which acts at both GIP and GLP-1 receptors. It is an injectable medicine. Grouping these treatments under “GLP-1s” can be convenient, but it should not hide their differences.

Here is how the routine differs for these examples:

| Product | Ingredient and route | Basic schedule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wegovy tablets | Oral semaglutide | Once daily; specific fasting and water instructions |
| Foundayo | Oral orforglipron | Once daily, with or without food |
| Wegovy injection | Injected semaglutide | Once weekly |
| Zepbound | Injected tirzepatide | Once weekly |

Sources: [Wegovy prescribing information](https://www.novo-pi.com/wegovy.pdf), [Foundayo prescribing information](https://pi.lilly.com/us/foundayo-uspi.pdf), and [Zepbound prescribing information](https://pi.lilly.com/us/zepbound-uspi.pdf). This table describes routines, not dose equivalence or a complete list of treatments.

## A morning routine can become part of treatment

Wegovy tablets must be taken in the morning on an empty stomach with no more than four ounces of plain water. The instructions require waiting at least 30 minutes before food, other drinks, or other oral medicines.

Foundayo does not have that fasting requirement. It can be taken with or without food.

Imagine someone who takes several medicines with breakfast and leaves for work early. The difference could matter more than simply preferring a tablet. It is worth asking the pharmacist to fit the actual instructions into your routine before assuming that any oral option will be easy.

A weekly injection has a different set of practical tasks: learning the device, following storage instructions, and remembering the scheduled day.

## Milligrams are not a strength ranking

The number printed on one product cannot be used to calculate an equivalent amount of another. Different ingredients and formulations reach and act in the body differently.

Even when two products contain semaglutide, switching routes requires the product-specific prescribing plan. Do not substitute tablets for injections, combine them, or adjust amounts using an online conversion.

If you are considering a change because of travel, needle discomfort, side effects, or access, explain that reason. The prescriber can assess whether the proposed alternative is suitable and how a transition should work.

## A pill can still have substantial precautions

Avoiding an injection does not remove the possibility of gastrointestinal or other serious adverse effects. The FDA’s Foundayo announcement, for example, includes warnings about pancreatitis, dehydration-related kidney injury, severe digestive reactions, and other risks.

Foundayo also has specific interactions with other drugs, and its label advises additional or non-oral contraception for 30 days after starting and after each dose increase for people using oral contraceptives. Do not apply that interval to another product: the instructions differ.

All four examples in the table have important contraindications and warnings, including restrictions involving a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, a particular thyroid cancer, or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN 2). Pregnancy plans, breastfeeding, other medicines, and digestive conditions need a prescribing discussion.

## Compare the treatment that fits your situation

The easiest option to take is not necessarily the most appropriate one for your health, and a trial percentage from one study cannot establish which product would work best for you.

Ask what benefit is expected for your circumstances, what evidence supports that expectation, what follow-up is included, and what the complete cost will be. Check the exact product and formulation covered by any insurance or access arrangement.

You can have a strong preference about pills or injections. The best conversation starts with that preference and then checks whether the treatment behind it fits.
